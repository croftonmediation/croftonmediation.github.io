How I can help

Different situations. A focus on moving forward.

Conflict can affect family relationships, working lives and the wider community. Mediation and conflict coaching offer different kinds of support, depending on the people involved and what they need.

⌂

Family mediation

A supported conversation for family members who need to address difficult issues, improve communication or explore practical arrangements.

  • Family conflict and communication
  • Support for separating couples
  • Exploring options and shared decisions
↔

Workplace mediation

Impartial support for colleagues or teams experiencing tension, misunderstandings or ongoing disagreement at work.

  • Interpersonal and team conflict
  • Communication and working relationships
  • Constructive dialogue and next steps
◎

Community mediation

A structured opportunity for people in a community to talk through a dispute, hear different perspectives and consider practical ways forward.

  • Neighbourhood or community disputes
  • Understanding different perspectives
  • Working toward workable agreements
✳

Interpersonal conflict coaching

One-to-one coaching to help you make sense of a conflict, consider your options and prepare for a challenging conversation.

  • Conflict in family or personal relationships
  • Workplace communication challenges
  • Building confidence and choosing next steps
My approach

Calm, impartial and focused on what comes next.

Conflict can leave people feeling stuck, unheard or unsure how to proceed. My role is to support a more constructive process, without taking sides or deciding the outcome for you.

1

Space to be heard

Make room for each person's perspective and help the conversation feel more manageable.

2

Clarity and understanding

Identify the issues that matter, explore differences and clarify what may be possible.

3

Practical next steps

Focus on options and constructive steps that reflect the needs and circumstances of those involved.

“A difficult conversation can be the beginning of a better understanding.”
